STATE AND TERRITORY NOMINATION — OVERVIEW FOR 190 AND 491
==========================================================
Last updated: May 2026
Sources: Department of Home Affairs allocations announcement
November 2025, individual state migration program pages
(Live in Melbourne, Migration NSW, Migration SA, etc.).

State and territory nomination is required for the Subclass
190 Skilled Nominated visa and most pathways to the Subclass
491 Skilled Work Regional visa. This file gives an overview
of how the system works and what each state was allocated
for the 2025-26 program year.


HOW STATE NOMINATION WORKS
---------------------------
Each Australian state and territory runs its own skilled
migration program. They independently decide:
- Which occupations they will nominate.
- What criteria they require (e.g., commitment to live and
  work in the state, prior work experience in the state,
  minimum points score).
- How and when they invite applicants.

The federal Department of Home Affairs allocates each state
a CAP on how many nominations they can issue per program
year (1 July – 30 June). Once that cap is reached, no more
nominations until the next program year.

You generally interact with TWO systems:
1. SkillSelect (federal) — submit an EOI here, ticking which
   states you are interested in being nominated by.
2. The state's own portal — submit a Registration of Interest
   (ROI) or formal nomination application.

Some states use ROI systems and select candidates from those;
some invite directly from SkillSelect.

WHAT EACH STATE TYPICALLY PRIORITISES
--------------------------------------
Each state's priorities shift annually based on labour market
needs. The 2025-26 patterns roughly look like this:

Victoria (VIC):
- Largest 190 allocation.
- Priorities include health, education, engineering, and IT.
- Selective and competitive; usually requires prior work or
  study connection to Victoria.
- Operated through the Live in Melbourne portal.

New South Wales (NSW):
- Large allocation.
- Structured selection emphasising skilled employment
  prospects.
- 491 nomination has three pathways: employed in regional
  NSW, invited by Investment NSW, or graduated from regional
  NSW.

South Australia (SA):
- Prioritises applicants currently living and working in
  South Australia.
- Offshore applicants assessed directly from SkillSelect.
- Offers Designated Area Migration Agreement (DAMA)
  pathways for specific occupations.

Queensland (QLD):
- Significantly increased nomination places for 2025-26.
- Focus on regional centres and priority industries.
- Uses an ROI system.

Western Australia (WA):
- Large allocation, major round held December 2025.
- Strong demand for healthcare, engineering, and trades.

Tasmania (TAS):
- Weekly invitation rounds.
- Strong demand for healthcare, hospitality, and trades.
- Smaller economy but historically more accessible nominations.

ACT (Canberra):
- Uses the Canberra Matrix points system (separate from
  the federal points test).
- Considers commitment to ACT (residence, study, employment
  there).

NT (Northern Territory):
- Steady, moderate allocation.
- Historically more accessible nominations but smaller
  economy and labour market.


COMMON ELIGIBILITY THEMES ACROSS STATES
----------------------------------------
Most states share a similar baseline before applying their
own filters:
- Under 45 at the time of invitation.
- Positive skills assessment in an eligible occupation.
- At least Competent English.
- Minimum 60-65 points on SkillSelect EOI (before state
  nomination points are added).
- Genuine commitment to live and work in the nominating
  state for at least 2 years (for 190) or the duration of
  the 491 (for 491, this is a legal visa condition).

Additional state-specific filters often include:
- Currently living and/or working in the state.
- Prior study in the state.
- A job offer in the state.
- Family ties in the state.


STRATEGY: APPLY TO MULTIPLE STATES IN PARALLEL
-----------------------------------------------
You can tick multiple states in SkillSelect AND submit ROIs
to multiple state programs simultaneously. Most applicants
do this to maximise their chances. Each state's process is
independent.

If you are nominated by one state, you generally cannot be
nominated by another for the same visa in the same program
year.


REGIONAL AREAS — WHAT COUNTS FOR 491
-------------------------------------
For Subclass 491 purposes, "regional Australia" includes
everywhere EXCEPT the metropolitan areas of:
- Sydney
- Melbourne
- Brisbane

This means most of Australia counts as regional, including:
- All of Adelaide, Perth, Hobart, Darwin, Canberra.
- Gold Coast, Sunshine Coast, Wollongong, Newcastle.
- All non-metropolitan parts of every state.

If you study or work outside the three excluded metros,
you may be earning 491 nomination eligibility automatically.


PRACTICAL TIPS FOR MELBOURNE-BASED STUDENTS
--------------------------------------------
- If you are studying in Melbourne, you are NOT in a regional
  area for 491 purposes. Studying in regional Victoria
  (Geelong, Ballarat, Bendigo) would count.
- Victoria's 190 program is competitive but has the largest
  national allocation. Strong English, post-study work
  experience in Victoria, and a Victorian occupation list
  match are key.
- Consider neighboring states (Tasmania, ACT, regional NSW
  or SA) if Victoria's lists do not include your occupation.
