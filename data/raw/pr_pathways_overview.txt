PR PATHWAYS FOR INTERNATIONAL STUDENTS IN AUSTRALIA — OVERVIEW
===============================================================
Last updated: May 2026
Sources: Department of Home Affairs (immi.homeaffairs.gov.au)
SkillSelect, Home Affairs visa listings.

This document is an orientation map. It shows how international
students typically move from the Student visa (subclass 500) to
permanent residency in Australia. Each named visa subclass has
its own dedicated file with detail; this file explains how they
fit together.


THE TYPICAL STUDENT-TO-PR JOURNEY
----------------------------------
The standard journey for an international student aiming for
permanent residency looks like this:

    [Subclass 500]   →   [Subclass 485]   →   [PR Visa]
     Student visa       Temporary Graduate    189 / 190 / 491
                                              (or 482 → 186)

There is no single "best" pathway. Which permanent residency
visa is right for you depends on:
- Your nominated occupation (whether it is on the relevant
  skilled occupation lists).
- Your points score (age, English, qualifications, work
  experience).
- Whether you have an Australian employer willing to sponsor
  you.
- Whether you are willing to live in a regional area.
- Your timeline and risk tolerance.


PATHWAY 1: 500 → 485 → 189 (POINTS-TESTED, NO SPONSORSHIP)
-----------------------------------------------------------
Subclass 189 Skilled Independent visa.
- Direct permanent residency, no sponsorship required.
- Strictly points-tested via SkillSelect.
- Occupation must be on the Medium and Long-term Strategic
  Skills List (MLTSSL).
- Theoretical minimum to submit an Expression of Interest is
  65 points, but in 2025-26, competitive scores are typically
  85-95+ depending on the occupation.
- Annual program is small and highly selective.
- Best for: top-scoring applicants in high-demand occupations
  (some healthcare, engineering, IT roles).

For most students, the 189 is not realistic without significant
points from age, English, qualifications, and work experience.
The 485 visa is often used to accumulate points (Australian
work experience, Professional Year, etc.) before applying.


PATHWAY 2: 500 → 485 → 190 (STATE-NOMINATED PR)
------------------------------------------------
Subclass 190 Skilled Nominated visa.
- Permanent residency from grant.
- Requires nomination by an Australian state or territory.
- State nomination adds 5 points to your score.
- Occupation must be on the MLTSSL or STSOL, plus the
  nominating state's specific occupation list.
- Around 12,850 places allocated nationally for 2025-26.
- Includes a two-year commitment to live in the nominating
  state.
- Best for: applicants whose occupation is in demand in a
  specific state but not competitive enough for 189.


PATHWAY 3: 500 → 485 → 491 → 191 (REGIONAL PROVISIONAL → PR)
-------------------------------------------------------------
Subclass 491 Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) visa.
- Five-year provisional visa, NOT immediate PR.
- Requires state OR eligible family member nomination.
- State/family nomination adds 15 points (more than 190).
- Must live and work in a designated regional area
  (which includes most of Australia except Sydney, Melbourne,
  and Brisbane).
- Around 7,500 places allocated nationally for 2025-26.
- Leads to permanent residency via the Subclass 191 visa
  after meeting requirements (typically 3 years of regional
  residence and minimum income).
- Best for: applicants willing to commit to regional Australia
  in exchange for easier nomination.

Important for Melbourne-based students: Melbourne itself is
NOT a regional area for 491 purposes. Designated regional
areas in Victoria include Geelong, Ballarat, Bendigo, and
most of regional Victoria, but you would need to relocate.


PATHWAY 4: 500 → 485 → 482 → 186 (EMPLOYER-SPONSORED)
-------------------------------------------------------
Subclass 482 Skills in Demand visa (employer-sponsored temporary)
→ Subclass 186 Employer Nomination Scheme visa (permanent).

The 482 (formerly TSS) was renamed Skills in Demand on
7 December 2024 with three new streams:
- Core Skills stream: occupation on Core Skills Occupation
  List (CSOL), salary AUD $76,515+.
- Specialist Skills stream: any ANZSCO occupation, salary
  AUD $141,210+, 7-day processing.
- Labour Agreement / Essential Skills stream.

Pathway:
- Find an Australian employer willing to sponsor you.
- Employer must be an approved Standard Business Sponsor.
- Work in the nominated occupation for 2 years.
- Transition to permanent residency via Subclass 186 under
  the Temporary Residence Transition (TRT) stream.
- Reduced from the previous 3-year work requirement.

Best for: applicants with a genuine job offer in their
nominated occupation. Bypasses the points test entirely.


COMPARING THE PATHWAYS AT A GLANCE
-----------------------------------
- Fastest to PR (if eligible): 189 or 190 (immediate PR on grant).
- Easiest to get nominated: 491 (regional, more places relative
  to demand, +15 points vs +5 for 190).
- No points test needed: 482 → 186 (employer-sponsored).
- No location commitment: 189 (the only truly unrestricted PR).

Most international students pursue MULTIPLE pathways in
parallel — submitting a 189 EOI while also seeking state
nomination for 190 or 491, and exploring employer sponsorship
opportunities through their 485 work rights.


COMMON SEQUENCE OF EVENTS
--------------------------
A typical timeline for someone aiming at points-tested PR:

Year 1-2:  Study on subclass 500.
Year 2-3:  Complete degree, apply for subclass 485.
Year 3-5:  Use 485 work rights to:
           - Gain Australian work experience (points).
           - Consider Professional Year (5 points).
           - Improve English test scores (more points).
           - Get skills assessment (mandatory for points-tested).
           - Submit EOI in SkillSelect.
           - Seek state nomination.
Year 4-6:  Apply for 189 / 190 / 491 if invited.
Year 5+:   Permanent residency, citizenship eligible after
           4 years of lawful residence.


KEY TERMS YOU WILL SEE
-----------------------
- SkillSelect: the online system for submitting Expressions of
  Interest (EOIs) for skilled visas.
- EOI: Expression of Interest. Free to submit. Does not
  guarantee an invitation.
- ITA: Invitation to Apply. Issued by the Department of Home
  Affairs based on points and occupation. 60 days to lodge
  the actual visa application.
- Skills assessment: a positive assessment by the relevant
  assessing authority (e.g., ACS for IT, Engineers Australia,
  VETASSESS for general professional occupations) confirming
  your qualifications meet Australian standards. Mandatory
  for points-tested visas.
- MLTSSL: Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List.
- STSOL: Short-term Skilled Occupation List.
- CSOL: Core Skills Occupation List (for the new 482 SID
  Core Skills stream).
- ANZSCO: Australian and New Zealand Standard Classification
  of Occupations. The catalogue of occupations and their codes.
