STUDENT VISA (SUBCLASS 500) — DOCUMENT CHECKLIST
=================================================
Last updated: May 2026
Sources: Department of Home Affairs Document Checklist Tool
(immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/web-evidentiary-tool),
Genuine Student requirement page, Study Australia.

This is a consolidated checklist. The exact documents you
need depend on your country of origin, course type, and
personal circumstances. Always run the official Document
Checklist Tool on the Department of Home Affairs website
for your specific case.


1. CORE IDENTITY AND ENROLMENT DOCUMENTS
-----------------------------------------
Valid passport:
- Must be valid for the entire visa period.
- Bio-data page is uploaded to ImmiAccount.

Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE):
- Issued by your CRICOS-registered Australian education
  provider via the PRISMS system after you accept your
  offer and pay any required tuition deposit.
- A Letter of Offer is NOT sufficient — only a CoE counts.
- One CoE per course in your study package.
- The CoE confirms course name, AQF level, start date,
  duration, and tuition fee.

Onshore applicants: from 1 January 2025, all onshore
student visa applications must include a valid CoE at the
time of lodgement.

Photographs:
- Recent passport-style photo (45mm x 35mm) on a white
  background, meeting Australian visa specifications.


2. GENUINE STUDENT (GS) REQUIREMENT
------------------------------------
The GS requirement replaced the Genuine Temporary Entrant
(GTE) requirement on 23 March 2024. All Subclass 500
applications lodged on or after that date are assessed
under GS.

You answer four mandatory questions directly inside the
ImmiAccount application form. Maximum 150 words per
response, in English. Do NOT attach a separate "GS
statement" PDF — the Department prefers responses in the
form fields, with supporting evidence attached separately.

The four GS questions cover:
1. Your current circumstances — ties to family, community,
   employment, and economic situation in your home country.
2. Why you chose this specific course and education
   provider in Australia, and your understanding of the
   course requirements and life in Australia.
3. How completing the course will benefit you personally
   and professionally (in your home country, in Australia,
   or another country).
4. Any other relevant information you want to provide.

A fifth question applies if you have previously held a
student visa or are applying onshore from a non-student visa.

Important: GS recognises that genuine students may
develop skills Australia needs and later apply for
permanent residence. Future PR intent does NOT count
against you under GS, unlike under the old GTE test.

Supporting evidence to upload alongside GS responses:
- Academic transcripts and completion certificates from
  previous study.
- Course history if you have studied in Australia before
  (with reasons for any provider changes).
- Current employment evidence: employer letter, payslips,
  contact details for verification.
- Evidence of ties to home country: family, property,
  business, community involvement.
- Research you did into your chosen course/provider:
  comparison notes, correspondence with the provider,
  open day attendance.


3. FINANCIAL EVIDENCE
----------------------
You must demonstrate genuine access to enough funds to
cover tuition, living costs, schooling for any children,
and return travel for your first 12 months.

Acceptable evidence (one or a combination):
- Personal bank statements covering at least the past
  3–6 months.
- Education loan approval letter from a registered
  financial institution.
- Scholarship or government sponsorship documentation.
- Term deposit certificates.
- Sponsor's financial documents accompanied by a
  statutory declaration and proof of relationship.

Annual income alternative: instead of available funds,
you can demonstrate that your parents or partner earn:
- AUD $72,465 per year (single applicant), or
- AUD $84,543 per year (with family members in
  application).
Supported by official tax documents.

The Department assesses both whether the funds exist
and whether you have genuine access to them.


4. ENGLISH LANGUAGE EVIDENCE
-----------------------------
Required unless you qualify for an exemption.

Common minimums (your education provider may require
higher scores):
- IELTS Academic: 6.0 overall (5.5 if packaged with
  ELICOS).
- PTE Academic: 50.
- TOEFL iBT: 64 (must be a secure test centre — home
  editions are not accepted).
- Cambridge English / OET: provider-equivalent scores.

Test result must be from within the past two years.

Common exemptions:
- Passport holder of UK, USA, Canada, New Zealand, or
  Republic of Ireland.
- Five years of full-time study in English in eligible
  countries.
- Other exemptions listed in the Migration (English
  Language Tests and Evidence Exemptions for Subclass
  500) Instrument.


5. OVERSEAS STUDENT HEALTH COVER (OSHC)
----------------------------------------
- Mandatory for the entire visa period.
- Coverage must start from your arrival date in Australia.
- Upload the OSHC certificate showing insurer name,
  policy number, and coverage start/end dates.
- Approved providers include Bupa, Medibank, Allianz,
  ahm, and nib.


6. ACADEMIC AND EMPLOYMENT DOCUMENTS
-------------------------------------
- Certified copies of all degrees, diplomas, and
  transcripts from previous study.
- NAATI-accredited translations for any non-English
  documents.
- Employment evidence if you've worked: employer
  letters, payslips, role descriptions.
- Resume / CV showing study and work history.


7. SECONDARY APPLICANTS (PARTNER AND CHILDREN)
-----------------------------------------------
If you include family members:
- Marriage certificate or evidence of de facto
  relationship (12+ months of cohabitation, joint
  finances, joint social recognition).
- Birth certificates for dependent children.
- Custody arrangements or consent letters from the
  non-travelling parent for any children under 18 not
  travelling with both parents.
- Each secondary applicant needs their own GS dependent
  responses.


8. HEALTH AND CHARACTER
------------------------
Health examination:
- Required for some applicants depending on country of
  origin and intended stay length.
- Booked through an approved panel physician.
- Results go directly from the physician to the
  Department — you do not upload them yourself.

Character documents:
- Police clearance / penal certificate from each country
  you have lived in for 12+ months in the past 10 years.
- Statutory declaration regarding character if requested.

Biometrics:
- Some applicants must visit a Visa Application Centre
  (VAC) for fingerprints and photo. VACs operate in
  major cities globally.


9. COMMON REASONS FOR REFUSAL
------------------------------
- Insufficient or unverifiable financial evidence.
- Inconsistencies between GS responses and supporting
  documents.
- Generic or AI-sounding GS responses with no specifics.
- Course choice that does not align with academic or
  employment background and lacks clear explanation.
- Excessive focus on migration outcomes rather than
  study intent.
- Missing CoE for any course in a study package.


10. PRACTICAL TIPS
-------------------
- Begin gathering documents 9–12 months before your
  course start date.
- Use clear, consistent file names (e.g.,
  NAME_Passport.pdf, NAME_CoE.pdf).
- Scan colour copies at 300 DPI minimum.
- Cross-check that every document supports the same
  narrative as your GS responses.
- Always use the official Document Checklist Tool on the
  Department of Home Affairs website for your specific
  case — it tailors the list to your circumstances.
